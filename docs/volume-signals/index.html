Buy zone: weekly KDJ K washed out, deep in its low band (the ETF is oversold on the weekly chart). A zone starts on the first washed-out close after a couple of weeks out of the band. Its record counts each zone once, from that first close: 1 and 3 months later, and the lowest close within the 3 months (how much further it fell first). Caution: a yellow candle late in the move in the last two weeks. Capitulation (magenta candles): a sharp one-day fall on very heavy volume; the card shows the latest one in the last two weeks and what followed past ones, against other sharp falls on lighter volume.

Average volume: the mean of about a month of sessions before the day. Candles and volume bars are coloured by what the day's volume did: yellow: a close above the highest high of the past month on light volume, well under average (the "low volume breakout" candle); high-volume breakout: the same close on heavy volume, well over average; capitulation: a sharp fall on very heavy volume; hollow: very light volume.

Low volume : a full session with less volume than every full session of the year before it. "Lowest since" is the latest earlier full session with as little volume, in shares and in dollars (shares × close). Half days never count either way.

Today so far: during US hours, volume against the average of recent sessions up to the same time of day ("pace"); 1.0× is a normal day. Shares are scaled to the daily totals. The day's candle is drawn faded and marked so far; the tables and the after-numbers use closed sessions only.

Volume by price: intraday bars over the last 3, 6 or 12 months, each bar's volume spread evenly over its high–low range, drawn on the right of the price chart. The busiest price is marked, with the band holding most of the volume around it. Low-volume zones (shaded): a run of prices where little traded next to the busiest price, wide enough to matter, with plenty of volume both above and below.

Weekly K: KDJ on weekly candles, with this week counted up to the day; during US hours it uses today's price so far. Late in the move = weekly K high in its upper band. First breakout out of a correction = the ETF was in a correction (a deep drop from its 1-year closing high) at some point in the last three months, and this is its first close above the past month's high since the lowest close of that stretch (on any volume). The after table splits yellow candles both ways and shows an ordinary day (every full session) for comparison.

Forward record: every yellow candle from Oct 19, 2026 on, logged with its weekly K once the next session has closed (so the day's volume has settled) and never re-scored; 1 week and 1 month are filled in as the prices arrive. The counts take one yellow per cluster, as the after table does; a day that stops being a yellow after a data fix stays listed, struck through, and leaves the counts.

Is it just SPY? SPY's shares and dollars, the S&P 500 ETF trio (SPY + VOO + IVV, dollars), and all US stock volume (NYSE's published consolidated tape totals, from 2019), each against its own average of the past month.

After past signals: the ETF's move 1 week, 1 month and 3 months after the signal day's close, the worst close within a month, and for breakouts the share whose high was still standing two weeks later. A repeat of the same signal within a week counts once. Compared with: other breakouts to a one-month high (yellow, high-volume breakout), other sharp falls on lighter volume (capitulation), every full session (low volume). Tags mark half days, bond-market holidays, the sessions around market holidays, options expiry, the quarterly expiry with the S&P rebalance, and the Russell reconstitution.

Daily and intraday ETF bars; all-US stock volume from NYSE's published totals.
