Days on record

Opened. New option contracts: the rise in open interest (contracts still open after each session) on one side, calls or puts. “Added” counts only series that are still open; an expiry that has passed drops out.

Net and of its book. Net = calls opened minus puts opened, after the contracts closed on each side. “Of its book” = that net as a share of every option contract open on the stock, so a big name and a small one can sit on the same list.

One-sided. How much of the new contracts went to the leaning side. A name ranks when its build is one-sided, big against its own book, and seen on most of the window's sessions.

Read. Where the late trades of the new contracts sat against the closing quote: near the ask reads as bought, near the bid as sold (sellers opened them). It is a lean, not a fact: every option has a buyer and a seller.

Build chip and gold line. The build chip counts the leaning side's new contracts and the expiries they went into; the gold line names the expiry, or the two expiries, that took most of them.

$ opened. The new contracts on the leaning side × each option's closing mid price × 100, added up over the window.

Held calls : puts. Every open call against every open put on the stock now, all strikes and expiries. The white tick, where shown, marks the call share at the window start.

King node, pile, negative king. The king node is the strike with the most call gamma (gamma dollars per 1% move); a pile is a run of neighbouring strikes holding most of it; the negative king is the strike with the most negative gamma.

Day strip and glyph. The day strip has one cell per session: green when calls led that day, red when puts led, an outline when it was quiet. The glyph shows where the window’s new contracts sit against the price: calls above the line, puts below it, nearer strikes in the middle and farther ones out at the ends.

Sorting. Every column sorts: a first click puts the highest (or A to Z, or the soonest) first, a second click flips it, “#” returns to the ranking. Rank numbers always stay the ranking's.

Timing. Open interest is posted the morning after each session, so the newest settle is one session behind and fills in over the next day. The Tape chip uses the newest session's trading: where more contracts traded than were open that morning, the difference must be new.
