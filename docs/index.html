What happened after each regime?

A different question: how often did simply holding each ETF produce a positive return?

Correlation compares the prior-session MII score with the later ETF return: +1 means they rise together perfectly, 0 means no linear relationship, and −1 means they move oppositely.

How to compare: “All eligible dates” is the baseline for the same period. A positive-return frequency of 65% means the ETF finished higher in 65 out of 100 observed starting dates; it is not a forecast or a strategy win rate. N counts overlapping starting dates, so many observations share the same market move. The paper’s 63% for LOW mean reversion answers a different question.

Mean reversion: expect an unusually stretched move to drift back toward its usual level. Breakout: look for price to leave an established range. Trend following: expect an established move to continue. The paper reports these patterns; this page does not run those trading rules or establish their future success.

Timing. The colored bands and hover show that date’s closing MII label. Outcome tables use the previous session’s MII score and label, then measure adjusted close on the displayed starting date to adjusted close 5, 20 or 60 ETF trading sessions later. This keeps the signal earlier than entry. The range selector filters starting dates. An outcome is included only when its full horizon is observed by the end of this saved dataset.

Fair baseline. Both baseline and regime groups use only dates with a known prior MII label and a complete forward return. Warmup and unfinished outcomes are excluded. “Positive” means strictly above zero. N is the number of overlapping starting dates, not independent trades. Tables describe association, with no trading costs, position rules or proof of an edge.

Two separate datasets. MII remains the existing nine-asset proxy calculation above; SPY and QQQ are a price comparison and do not replace its inputs. ETF prices are Yahoo Finance adjusted closes (splits and dividends), retrieved 30 Sep 2026 at 03:14 UTC, with no price filling. The paper rates use the author’s study, not this proxy dataset. ELI5 guide to the numbers

Raw versus scaled. A raw value is the original measurement, such as average correlation. Scaling puts different measurements on a 0–1 range so they can be combined. A scaled 0.30 does not mean 30% correlation or a 30% chance of profit.

Calibration. The model learns the earlier low and high of each measurement. New extremes can widen that range. The first valid reading uses 0.5 while the range begins forming; that is a starting value, not proof of neutral conditions.

Warmup. A calculation first needs 252 daily returns. Regime labels then need 252 earlier daily MII observations and the next monthly cutoff update. These observations include scores held between scheduled calculations.

LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH. The labels compare the score with its recent past. Proposed links to trend following, breakouts and mean reversion remain research hypotheses, not demonstrated trade recommendations.

Snapshot, method and source details

Saved dataset. Yahoo Finance daily unadjusted Close, 5 Jan 2015 through 29 Sep 2026. Retrieved 30 Sep 2026 at 00:18 UTC. This is a public proxy panel, not the paper’s original licensed dataset.

Markets. SPX, NDX, EURO STOXX 50 and E-mini S&P futures supply Memory and Information. The Network group also includes VIX, XLK, XLF, XLV and XLE.

Carried prices. 83 price cells use explicitly recorded earlier closes. Some final inputs are from 28 Sep even though the table ends 29 Sep. Local exchange dates do not imply simultaneous information availability.

Calculation. Nine original features; 252-return window, 21-session schedule, expanding scaling. Monthly historical terciles use up to 756 prior daily MII rows, with a fixed 252-row warmup.

Provenance is the receipt. The saved prices, source dates, fill report and checksums are kept with the project’s saved data. Fingerprints detect file changes; they do not prove a data provider is correct.
